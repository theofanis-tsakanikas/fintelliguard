## Customer due diligence (AML)

Obliged entities must apply customer due diligence measures when carrying out an
occasional transaction that amounts to EUR 15,000 or more, or that appears to be linked
to money laundering. Enhanced due diligence applies to higher-risk situations, including
transactions that are unusually large or follow an unusual pattern with no apparent
economic or lawful purpose.

## Transaction monitoring (AML)

Obliged entities must monitor transactions undertaken throughout the course of the
business relationship to ensure consistency with the customer's risk profile. Sudden
velocity spikes, geographic inconsistency between the customer's usual location and the
transaction origin, and atypical hours of activity are recognised indicators warranting
review and, where suspicion persists, a suspicious activity report.

## Strong customer authentication (PSD2)

Payment service providers must apply strong customer authentication where the payer
initiates an electronic payment transaction. Transaction risk analysis may exempt
low-risk transactions, but a mismatch between the device or country of origin and the
customer's established profile raises the risk score and should remove the exemption.

## Documented rationale

Each flagged transaction must carry a documented justification referencing the specific
control or article relied upon, the indicators observed, and the recommended action
(allow, review, or block), to support audit and regulatory reporting obligations.
